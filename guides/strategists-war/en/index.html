How to Play Strategists’ War

Strategists’ War is a real-time strategy game set in an age before firearms, played from the position of the commanding strategist. There is no resource gathering and no base building. Battles are decided by the formations you assemble before the fighting starts, and by where you choose to send your squads once it does.

How you win

A battle lasts five minutes. There are two ways to win.

  • Kill the enemy strategist. The battle ends the moment it happens.
  • When five minutes run out, the side with more surviving soldiers wins. Equal numbers is a draw.

What is easy to miss is that survivors are counted as units. A cavalryman costs twice as much and is far stronger, but in the final count he is one body, exactly like a single infantryman. If you intend to win on time, cheap infantry serve you better; if you intend to end it early, invest in mobility. That trade-off is the heart of army building.

Three steps from preparation to battle

1. Build a squad

On the FORMATION screen you place soldiers on an 11×11 grid to build a squad. Each squad may spend up to 20 cost. Infantry cost 1 and cavalry cost 2, so a squad is at most 20 infantry or 10 cavalry. You can save up to 20 squads.

Eight formations are included from the first launch: Wedge, Phalanx, Testudo, Shieldwall, Line, Column, Square and Crescent. Taking them into battle as they are is a good way to get a feel for the game.

2. Build an army

An army is up to ten of your squads placed on a 7×7 grid. The lower the row number, the further forward that squad deploys. You may use the same squad as many times as you like, and you do not need all ten — one squad is enough to march out.

Do not forget to place your strategist. Choose one of the squads and put the strategist on an empty cell within it; until you do, the army cannot be deployed.

3. Fight

On the battlefield, selecting a squad and telling it where to go is the only thing you do. Attacking happens automatically. Touch (or click) a squad and swipe (or drag) to the place you want it to go. Pinch or use the wheel to zoom.

Five rules that decide battles

Flanks take double, backs take triple

Damage depends on the angle of the attack. The 45 degrees to either side of straight ahead count as the front, anything beyond 135 degrees counts as the rear, and the rest is the flank. Attacks from the flank deal double damage, and attacks from the rear deal triple. Getting cavalry around the side or back of a squad that is locked in a frontal fight changes the outcome completely, with no extra troops. The reverse is just as true: leaving your own squads with their backs to the enemy is the most dangerous thing you can do.

A squad moves at the speed of its slowest soldier

Squads hold their formation and move as one body, so squad speed equals the lowest speed among its members. Mix a single shieldbearer at speed 0.8 into a squad of cavalry at 3.0 and the whole squad walks at 0.8. The strategist has a fixed speed of 1.0 and is included in that calculation, so a cavalry squad carrying the strategist drops to 1.0. If you want a squad to be fast, keep it pure.

Combat starts on its own

A waiting squad engages automatically when an enemy enters its longest weapon range, or comes within a distance of 5.0. A squad containing bows reacts at range 8, which means it can stop far earlier than you intended. When you want to push the front line forward, moving your archers and your melee squads separately is much easier to control.

Arrows can miss

An arrow flies straight toward the point it was aimed at when it was loosed. It does not track. If an enemy is within 0.5 cells of where it lands, it hits; otherwise it misses. In other words, fast-moving cavalry can outrun arrows. Bows are at their best against a stationary, tightly packed enemy. There is no line-of-sight blocking, so archers can shoot over the heads of your own troops.

Formations remember their facing

When you LOCK a soldier's facing on the formation screen, that angle is stored relative to the front of the squad. In battle it is applied against whichever way the squad is currently facing, so formations that point soldiers outward in every direction — Square, Testudo — keep their all-round defence even after they move and turn.

Tips for building an army

  • Keep the strategist out of the front. If the strategist falls, you lose immediately. Assigning them to a rear squad and rarely moving it is the safe play. That said, soldiers in the strategist's own squad attack at double strength, so deliberately placing them with your main force to force an early decision is a real option.
  • Use shieldbearers as a wall. 200 HP is double anyone else's, but their attack is only 5. Shieldbearers in front with swordsmen or spearmen behind splits the job of absorbing damage from the job of dealing it.
  • Spearmen win on reach. Range 1.5 beats a sword's 1.0, so spearmen land the first blow against a packed enemy. Their 1.5-second interval is slow, so they work better in a line than trading blows alone.
  • Cavalry are for going around. Speed 3.0 is three times an infantryman's. Their real job is not the frontal charge but the wide sweep into the enemy's rear.
  • Do not throw away bodies. Because the clock is decided on survivor count, squads of cost-1 infantry carry real weight in a battle that goes the distance.

Playing against others

SINGLE PLAY gives you battles of two to four players against AI. In MULTI PLAY you can share the six-character room code you are issued, or use random matching to be paired automatically with someone who is waiting. Four-player battles can also be played as 2-vs-2 teams. In a team battle you keep playing even if your ally's strategist falls, but your side's movement speed drops by 20%, and you lose once both strategists are down.

Add to Home Screen

On iPhone and Android, choose "Add to Home Screen" from the browser's share menu to play full-screen like an app. Pages you have opened once are stored on the device, so SINGLE PLAY works even without a signal (MULTI PLAY needs a connection).

On iPhone, Safari and the Home Screen app keep separate saved data. Squads, armies and your player name are not shared between them, and there is no way to carry them over. Once you have added the app, we recommend playing from there.

Formations saved while playing in Safari may be deleted by iOS if you do not visit the site for about a week. This is much less likely to happen with the Home Screen version.
